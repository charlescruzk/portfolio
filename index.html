04

Concept, generated.

A layer on top of retouching: I concept a campaign, generate it with AI from the brand's real garments, then finish every frame by hand. Drag to compare — left is the raw generation, right is my retouched, graded final.

Step 01

Idea & references

The concept starts with a moodboard and the brand's actual product. Flatlays and fit crops become the garment reference set — so what gets generated is the clothing the company actually sells, not an approximation.

Step 02

AI generation

Identity-consistent models are generated wearing those garments in the campaign setting. Location, wardrobe, casting, and light are art-directed by prompt — a creative pitch you can see, before anyone books a shoot.

Step 03

Retouch & grade

Every generation passes the same human bar as a real shoot: cleanup, garment fidelity checked against the real product, and a film-style color grade that sells the story.

05

Built for volume

Volume doesn't come from working faster — it comes from building a system that never makes the same decision twice.

The standard

I wrote the retouching SOP: repeatable decisions for color, skin, cleanup, and handoff, so quality doesn't depend on mood or memory.

The automation

Photoshop actions and droplets for repeat operations. Scripted batch exports that removed recorded-action limits. Naming and folder systems that make any asset findable in seconds, backed by a Drive-based recall and versioning system — tied together with custom internal tooling I coded with AI-assisted development.

The AI layer

AI belongs in the pipeline — scripting, automation, repetitive ops. It doesn't get final judgment on skin, color, or brand look. That stays human.

The result

600+ image weeks. One consistent brand look across three categories. External overflow QC'd to the same bar before it ships.
